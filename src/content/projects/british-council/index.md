problem:
  headline: Characters any young child, anywhere, could feel close to.
  text: >
    The British Council needed characters and an animation style for a
    series of bite-size animations for children aged 3 to 6, to be used
    in different regions of the world. They had to be engaging and
    interesting for very young children, and work across a wide variety
    of classrooms and cultural backgrounds.
  image: ./expressions.jpg
  imageAlt: Grid of 24 coloured squares in green, yellow, blue, purple and mint on deep indigo, each with a simple face drawn in dark purple lines showing a different expression

research:
  headline: Looking at classrooms around the world.
  text: >
    Working directly with the client, I looked at how schools look in
    different countries, using references such as The Guardian's photo
    series of schools around the world for UN World Teachers' Day. That
    shaped characters and environments that don't belong to any single
    place.
  insights:
    - Classrooms look very different from one country to the next.
    - Children aged 3–6 read faces and body language before words.
    - The fewer specific details, the more easily any child can relate to a character.

solution:
  headline: Simple characters built from the brand's shapes, where expression and body language do the talking.
  decisions:
    - title: Built from the brand's shapes
      text: >
        After several iterations, the characters were built from shapes
        taken from the British Council brand, in different sizes for
        children and adults, with only a few tacit characteristics such as
        glasses or tufts of hair.
      image: ./cast.jpg
      imageAlt: The cast in pastel colours, from three small round and square children, to three taller children with glasses or tufts of hair, to two tall adults, one with glasses
    - title: Expression does the talking
      text: >
        Keeping features minimal leaves facial expression and body language
        as the main way the characters communicate once animated, so a child
        in any part of the world can understand them.
      image: ./character-sheet-2.jpg
      imageAlt: Character sheet with front views of each character shape, blank versions without faces, and the teacher character in six poses
    - title: Exploring the style through iterations
      text: >
        We tried line, colour, pastel, hand-drawn and paper-cut treatments
        together to find the right balance of warmth and simplicity for very
        young children.
      image: ./finishes.jpg
      imageAlt: The same moment in three finishes, flat, paper-cut and watercolour, where a small character with a question mark speech bubble scratches its head while the tall teacher reaches out to help
    - title: Environments that fit any classroom
      text: >
        Simple desks, walls and boards, with no features from any one
        country, so the scenes feel familiar in classrooms everywhere.
      image: ./classroom.jpg
      imageAlt: Paper-cut style classroom with rows of white shape characters at desks, waving at a tall teacher character seen from behind

reflection: >
  This project taught me to adapt: finding a creative answer to a
  problem that had to be universal. It took several rounds of ideas and
  iterations to get there. The result looks simple, and that simplicity
  is exactly what lets any child, anywhere, connect with the characters.
