  role: Digital designer, responsible for the visual design, UI, graphics and animation style
  team: Learning designers, animators and a project manager

  tools: [Photoshop, Illustrator, Evolve]
  impact: A dense, technical subject made visual and easy to follow

problem:
  headline: Making a vast, technical industry easy to explore.
  text: >
    Lloyd's Register is a global maritime services provider, working in
    classification, compliance and advisory across the shipping industry.
    They needed a course introducing the fundamentals of the maritime
    world: the scale of the industry, its main sectors and actors, vessel
    types, value chains, new technology, and the challenges and
    opportunities ahead. It was published in Evolve, so every visual had
    to work within that tool while still feeling modern and immersive.
  image: ./course-topics.jpg
  imageAlt: Course menu with six topic cards, each with an aerial photo of ports, marinas or ships and a View button, one locked until the previous topic is complete

research:
  headline: Technical content, a fixed toolset and a need to feel immersive.
  text: >
    I worked from the course content, a wide range of topics with a lot
    of technical information, and from what Evolve could do as a
    publishing tool. My role was to turn that into a visual language that
    made the subject feel approachable and kept learners moving through
    the course.
  insights:
    - Technical content needed to feel approachable, not dense.
    - Every visual had to fit Evolve's layouts and components.
    - Six topics and an assessment needed one consistent style.

solution:
  headline: Aerial imagery, clear UI and diagrams that bring the maritime world close.
  decisions:
    - title: Backgrounds relevant to every topic
      text: >
        I created background images for each topic from aerial views of
        ships, ports and open water, so learners always feel at sea. They
        set the tone while leaving clear space for text and interactions.
      image: ./ship-scale.jpg
      imageAlt: Page over a deep blue sea background comparing the Empire State Building (381 metres) with the Ever Alot container ship (400 metres) in white line drawings, with an aerial photo of a tanker's bow
    - title: UI, graphics and diagrams as one system
      text: >
        I designed the UI and graphics to sit on the backgrounds as one
        system: cut-out ship images that learners select to explore each
        type of vessel, and diagrams that break down topics such as value
        chains, using a consistent set of icons, arrows and labels.
      image: ./ui-and-diagrams.jpg
      imageAlt: Two course screens on deep navy, four cut-out aerial photos of ships labelled dry cargo, liquid cargo, specialised cargo and others, and a diagram of how the marine fuel, shipbuilding and maritime operational value chains depend on each other
    - title: Style and assets for the animation
      text: >
        I created and designed the assets and visual style for the course
        animations, so the videos feel part of the same experience as the
        rest of the course.
      image: ./animation.jpg
      imageAlt: Animated video frame in flat illustration style showing people on a video call, a forklift, shipping containers and a woman presenting data
  showcase: ./challenges.jpg
  showcaseAlt: Challenges and opportunities page with seven teal icons, covering topics such as climate, people, security and energy, arranged around an aerial photo of a tanker

impact:
  headline: A technical subject turned into a visual course that met the client’s expectations and learning objectives.
  stats: []

reflection: >
  Every tool has limits. Working within Evolve showed me that creativity
  and flexibility can push a tool further than expected, finding
  solutions that feel fresh and immersive without being impossible to
  build.
