problem:
  headline: Two brands coming together, and content that needed to feel like one.
  text: >
    Cloud Academy is a cloud computing training platform based in San
    Francisco, acquired in 2019 by QA, a UK provider of technical
    training. As part of bringing the two companies together, Cloud
    Academy's content needed to align with the QA brand while keeping its
    own identity. Our team creates a large amount of content for the
    brand, so it needed a shared way of working to keep everything
    cohesive.
  image: ./logos.jpg
  imageAlt: Logo lockups for QA and for Cloud Academy "part of QA" on white, grey, dark teal and green backgrounds

research:
  headline: Three principles behind every decision.
  text: >
    I came up with the project and set out three design principles as the
    foundation of the guide. Every part of the system, from colour to
    characters, was checked against them.
  insights:
    - "Efficiency: everything should help the whole team work efficiently."
    - "Adaptability: everything should be adaptable, so it works in any kind of content."
    - "Simplicity: everything should be simple and bring the story to life."

solution:
  headline: A complete design system for content, from colour and type to illustration, characters and animation.
  decisions:
    - title: Colour aligned with QA
      text: >
        To bring Cloud Academy closer to QA, I set a simple primary blue as
        the base of all content, paired with QA's corporate colours and a
        vibrant secondary palette for illustrations and highlights.
      image: ./colour.jpg
      imageAlt: Secondary palette page with pairs of base and accent colours in orange, pink, purple, yellow and green, next to an illustration of a person working at a laptop inside a cloud shape
    - title: An illustration system made of reusable parts
      text: >
        Illustrations are simple and flat, built from elements such as
        devices, windows, connectors and brand shapes that can be edited
        and combined to explain any idea. They're easy to animate, and
        avoiding 3D means everything can be reused in future scenes.
      image: ./illustration.jpg
      imageAlt: Illustration style page with a person in an orange chair, a padlock, a password field, a server, a globe and clouds connected by dashed lines
    - title: A character pack
      text: >
        Characters follow the same rules: simple, flat and configurable, so
        they can show actions and personality on screen in any scene.
      image: ./characters.jpg
      imageAlt: Character pack with a diverse group of flat illustrated people working at desks, planning on a board, using phones and talking
    - title: Screen types for video and animation
      text: >
        I designed the animation style and a set of screen types for video,
        from title screens to key facts, so animated content could be
        produced quickly and look consistent.
      image: ./screen-types.jpg
      imageAlt: Orange video screen with "Billions" and "Internet of things" facts beside an illustration of a person lying on the floor with a laptop and floating app windows
  showcase: ./in-practice.jpg
  showcaseAlt: Six course screens using the style guide in purple, orange, green and yellow, including "Explore SharePoint Server Hybrid", "Who should attend this course?" and illustrated diagrams

    - image: ./icons.jpg
      imageAlt: Iconography page with a grid of white line icons for cloud, servers, security and devices on dark teal

impact:
  headline: One style guide the team used to keep Cloud Academy content consistent as the two companies came together.
  stats: []

reflection: >
  Merging two brands can be hard on the people living through it. Design
  gave everyone something concrete to work from: a shared visual
  language that helped two companies take the next step together, and
  let the team keep creating with confidence through the transition.
